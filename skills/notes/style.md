# Note style

These are the defaults for Library pages. Rules under "Learned from edits" come from the user's own edits and override the defaults.

## General

- Core points only: just the ideas the user needs to recall the source without rewatching it. Leave out supporting detail, asides and anecdotes, unless an anecdote is the best way to remember an idea. Don't summarise it minute by minute.
- Terse: short phrases rather than full prose. Cut any word that doesn't carry meaning.
- Use plain, specific UK English. No hype, no filler, no "In this video...".
- Group ideas by theme, not by the order they came up.
- Length: about 6–10 body bullets for a 20-minute talk. Grow it more slowly than the length of the source.

## Videos, podcasts and talks

1. Opening line: `Notes on [**Title — Speaker (Org)**](url) · Channel · NN min · Mon YYYY. Timestamps jump to that point in the video.`
2. A `<callout icon="💡">` headed **Top 3 takeaways**, followed by a numbered list of exactly three points, each under about 12 words. Put the core claim first. The row's Takeaway property is point 1 written as a sentence.
3. 2–4 `##` sections, each a theme. Name sections by what they argue ("Why the spec matters more than the code"), not "Part 1".
4. Bullets: a **bold lead stating the idea** in one line. Add at most one short clause of why or an example, only where the lead alone wouldn't make sense months later. End each bullet with a grey timestamp link: `<span color="gray">[02:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ID&t=161s)</span>`.
5. At most one `>` quote, for a line worth remembering word for word.
6. If the speaker gives concrete steps, add `## Putting it into practice` with a numbered list.
7. `## Resources`: things the source points to that are worth having, such as tools, repos, papers, books, people or other talks. Write one line each: a linked name, a dash, then what it is. Link only sources you've confirmed. Skip passing mentions, and leave the section out if there's nothing worth listing.
8. Finish with `---` and a grey italic "Where it fits:" line linking it to other Library rows or topics.

## Articles and papers

Same as videos, but without timestamps. Name the section of the source in brackets where that helps. For papers, add a short "Method and evidence" section and say what the evidence does and doesn't show.

## Discussions (recaps of conversations)

- Context: one line on what was being decided or explored.
- `## Decisions`: what was settled, with the reason for each.
- `## Reasoning`: the key arguments and trade-offs, including rejected options in one line each.
- `## Open questions / next steps`
